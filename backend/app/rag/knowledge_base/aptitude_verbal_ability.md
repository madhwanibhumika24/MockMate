# Verbal Ability Reference

## Synonyms & Antonyms
A synonym has nearly the same meaning; an antonym has the opposite meaning, so read the word in context first since many words carry more than one sense
Eliminate options that are clearly unrelated in tone or field before comparing the remaining close options
Watch for a distractor that is a synonym of the given word placed among antonym options, or the reverse
Prefixes are a useful clue: "in-", "un-", "dis-", and "non-" often, though not always, reverse a word's meaning

Worked example: Choose the antonym of "Benevolent" from Kind, Malevolent, Generous, Charitable.
"Benevolent" means kind or well-meaning, so its antonym is "Malevolent" (ill-meaning); the other three options are synonyms, not antonyms.

## Sentence Correction
Check subject-verb agreement first: a singular subject takes a singular verb, a plural subject takes a plural verb
Keep tenses consistent within a sentence unless the meaning clearly requires a shift
Watch for commonly confused pairs: "its" versus "it's", "their" versus "there" versus "they're", and "affect" versus "effect"
A modifier should sit next to the word it describes; a misplaced modifier changes or confuses the sentence's meaning
Read the corrected sentence back in full before finalizing, since an option can fix one error while introducing another

Worked example: Correct the sentence "Neither of the answers were correct."
"Neither" is singular, so it takes a singular verb: "Neither of the answers was correct."

## Reading Comprehension
Read the questions before a long passage, so you know what to look for while reading
Distinguish the passage's main idea, its overall point, from supporting details, the specific facts used to back it up
An inference question asks what the passage implies, not what it states directly, and the answer should still be strongly supported by the text
Watch for answer options that are true in general but not actually supported by this specific passage, since these are common distractors
Words like "always," "never," and "all" in an answer option are often too extreme to be supported by a real passage

Worked example: A passage explains that a company's profits rose because of a new product line, despite rising costs elsewhere. What can be inferred?
It can be inferred that the new product line's contribution to profit outweighed the impact of the rising costs elsewhere, even though the passage never states this directly.

## Para Jumbles
Find the opening sentence first; it usually introduces the topic without relying on a pronoun like "it," "this," or "they" that refers back to something earlier
Look for linking words such as however, therefore, similarly, or for example that show which sentence must follow another
A sentence that starts with a pronoun or a conjunction like "but" or "and" almost never opens the paragraph
Pair sentences that clearly link first, such as a cause and its effect, then slot that pair into the remaining sequence

Worked example: Arrange: (1) This made the roads unsafe. (2) Heavy rain hit the city yesterday. (3) As a result, several accidents were reported. (4) Visibility dropped sharply.
Sentence 2 introduces the event, 4 is a direct effect with no pronoun gap, 1 follows using "This" to refer to poor visibility, and 3 concludes with "As a result": the order is 2, 4, 1, 3.

## One-Word Substitution
Learn substitutions grouped by theme, such as people who do something, places for something, or fear of something, rather than as one long unrelated list
Many "fear of" and "study of" words follow Latin or Greek roots, such as "-phobia" for fear and "-logy" for study, and recognizing the root helps eliminate wrong options
Read the full phrase carefully, since a small wording change points to a different word entirely
When unsure, check whether an option is merely a synonym of a key word in the phrase rather than a true substitute for the whole phrase

Worked example: Choose the one word for "A person who knows many languages."
"Polyglot" -- from "poly-" meaning many and "-glot" meaning tongue or language.
