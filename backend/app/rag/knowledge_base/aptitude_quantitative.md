# Quantitative Aptitude Formula Reference

## Percentages
x% of N = (x / 100) * N
To express a fraction a/b as a percentage: (a / b) * 100
Percentage increase = (Increase / Original value) * 100
Percentage decrease = (Decrease / Original value) * 100
If a value increases by x% and later decreases by x%, the net change is always a decrease of (x^2 / 100)%
To find the original value before an x% increase, divide the new value by (1 + x/100). Before an x% decrease, divide by (1 - x/100).

Worked example: A shirt's price was increased by 20% to Rs. 600. What was the original price?
Original price = 600 / 1.20 = Rs. 500.

## Profit, Loss & Discount
Profit = Selling Price (SP) - Cost Price (CP), when SP is greater than CP
Loss = Cost Price (CP) - Selling Price (SP), when CP is greater than SP
Profit % = (Profit / CP) * 100
Loss % = (Loss / CP) * 100
Selling Price = CP * (1 + Profit% / 100), or CP * (1 - Loss% / 100)
Discount = Marked Price (MP) - Selling Price (SP)
Discount % = (Discount / MP) * 100
For two successive discounts of x% and y%, the effective single discount is: x + y - (xy / 100)

Worked example: A shop marks an item at Rs. 1000 and offers successive discounts of 10% and 20%. What is the final selling price?
Effective discount = 10 + 20 - (10*20/100) = 28%. Selling price = 1000 * (1 - 0.28) = Rs. 720.

## Time, Speed & Distance
Speed = Distance / Time
1 km/h = 5/18 m/s, and 1 m/s = 18/5 km/h
Average speed for equal distances at speeds a and b = 2ab / (a + b)
Relative speed in the same direction = difference of the two speeds; in opposite directions = sum of the two speeds
Time to cross a stationary object = length of train / speed of train
Boats and streams: Downstream speed = boat speed + stream speed; Upstream speed = boat speed - stream speed

Worked example: A train 150 m long crosses a pole in 15 seconds. What is its speed in km/h?
Speed = 150 / 15 = 10 m/s = 10 * (18/5) = 36 km/h.

## Ratio & Proportion
A ratio a:b can be scaled to ka:kb for any k, without changing its value
To split an amount N in the ratio a:b, the two parts are N * a/(a+b) and N * b/(a+b)
Proportion: a:b = c:d means a*d = b*c (product of extremes equals product of means)
Direct proportion: as one quantity increases, the other increases in the same ratio
Inverse proportion: as one quantity increases, the other decreases in the same ratio, so their product stays constant

Worked example: Divide Rs. 4500 between A and B in the ratio 4:5.
A's share = 4500 * 4/9 = Rs. 2000. B's share = 4500 * 5/9 = Rs. 2500.

## Simple & Compound Interest
Simple Interest (SI) = (Principal * Rate * Time) / 100
Amount under simple interest = Principal + SI
Compound Interest (CI), compounded annually: Amount = Principal * (1 + Rate/100)^Time
CI = Amount - Principal
For the same principal, rate, and time, CI is always greater than or equal to SI, and equal only when time is 1 year
When compounded half-yearly, use Rate/2 and Time*2 in the compound interest formula; adjust similarly for quarterly compounding

Worked example: Find the compound interest on Rs. 10,000 for 2 years at 10% per annum, compounded annually.
Amount = 10000 * (1.10)^2 = 10000 * 1.21 = Rs. 12,100. CI = 12100 - 10000 = Rs. 2,100.
